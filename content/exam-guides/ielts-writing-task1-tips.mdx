IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 is worth one-third of your Writing score. You have 20 minutes to write at least 150 words describing a visual — a line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table, map, or process diagram. Most test-takers lose marks by making the same 3 mistakes. Here's how to avoid them and score Band 7+.

## The 3-Part Structure That Works

Every Task 1 response should follow this structure:

**Paragraph 1 — Introduction (2 sentences)**
Paraphrase the question. Describe what the visual shows in your own words. Never copy the task wording.

**Paragraph 2 — Overview (2–3 sentences)**
Summarize the most important trends or features. Do NOT include specific numbers here. This is the "big picture" paragraph.

**Paragraph 3 (and 4) — Details**
Support the overview with specific data. Use numbers, percentages, and comparisons.

The overview is the most important paragraph. Examiners look for it specifically — missing it can drop your Task Achievement score by a full band.

## The Introduction: How to Paraphrase Correctly

Original task wording:
> *"The graph below shows the percentage of households in the UK with access to the internet from 2000 to 2020."*

Weak introduction (too close to original):
> "The graph shows the percentage of households in the UK with access to the internet from 2000 to 2020."

Strong introduction:
> "The line graph illustrates how internet access among UK households changed over a twenty-year period between 2000 and 2020."

Changes made:
- "graph" → "line graph" (more specific)
- "shows" → "illustrates"
- "percentage of households with access to the internet" → "internet access among households"
- "from 2000 to 2020" → "over a twenty-year period between 2000 and 2020"

## The Overview: What to Include

The overview describes the **main trends** — what stands out most. Ask yourself:
- What is the highest / lowest?
- What increased / decreased the most?
- Are there any significant turning points?
- What is the general direction (upward, downward, stable)?

Example overview for an internet access line graph:
> "Overall, internet access rose considerably across all age groups during this period, with the sharpest growth occurring between 2000 and 2010. Younger households consistently maintained the highest access rates throughout."

Note: no specific numbers. Save those for the detail paragraphs.

## Language for Describing Trends

### For increases:
- rose / increased / grew / climbed / surged
- there was a rise / an increase / a surge in...

### For decreases:
- fell / declined / dropped / decreased / dipped
- there was a fall / a drop / a decline in...

### For no change:
- remained stable / levelled off / plateaued / stayed constant at

### For speed/degree:
- **sharply / dramatically** (big change fast)
- **steadily / gradually** (slow, consistent change)
- **slightly / marginally** (small change)

### For comparisons:
- X was higher than Y
- X accounted for twice as much as Y
- X and Y showed similar trends

## Common Task 1 Mistakes

**1. Writing a conclusion**
Task 1 is a description, not an essay. There is no conclusion. The overview IS your summary. Don't write "In conclusion..." at the end.

**2. Describing every single data point**
Examiners want key features selected and grouped, not a list of every number. Group similar trends together.

**3. Writing fewer than 150 words**
Count carefully. Under 150 words loses Task Achievement marks automatically.

**4. Copying the question**
If you copy the task description word-for-word into your introduction, it is not counted toward your word total and signals poor vocabulary range.

**5. Giving personal opinions**
"I think the increase in internet access is very good for society." — this is wrong for Task 1. Describe what you see, don't evaluate it.

## A Band 7 Example Response

**Task:** *The bar chart shows the proportion of people in five age groups who used the internet daily in a European country in 2005 and 2015.*

---

The bar chart compares the frequency of daily internet use across five age categories in a European nation, measured at two points a decade apart.

Overall, daily internet usage rose significantly across all age groups between 2005 and 2015, with the most dramatic growth seen among older generations. Younger users already had high rates in 2005, leaving less room for growth.

In 2005, those aged 16–24 recorded the highest proportion of daily users at approximately 70%, followed closely by the 25–34 group at around 65%. By 2015, both figures had climbed to over 90%, representing a moderate increase. In contrast, the 55–64 and 65+ groups saw far sharper rises: the former jumped from roughly 20% to 60%, while the latter increased from just 10% to nearly 40%.

The 35–54 age group showed a steady upward trend, moving from approximately 45% in 2005 to around 80% in 2015.

**Word count:** 168 | **Estimated band:** 7.0–7.5

## Key Takeaways

- Always write an overview — it's the most important paragraph
- Paraphrase the introduction — never copy
- Use specific data in the detail paragraphs only
- Group trends together rather than listing every number
- Stay objective — describe, don't evaluate
