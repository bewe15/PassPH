**Read Aloud** is the first task in PTE Speaking and one of the most important. It contributes to both your **Speaking score** and your **Reading score** — making it worth more than any other single task in the exam. Getting it right can significantly lift your overall result.

## What Read Aloud Involves

You see a text on screen (up to 60 words). You have 30–40 seconds to prepare (a beep signals when to start). You then read it aloud into the microphone. The recording stops automatically.

PTE's AI scores you on:
- **Oral Fluency** — did you speak smoothly without long pauses or hesitations?
- **Pronunciation** — did you produce the sounds of English clearly?
- **Content** — did you read all the words correctly?

## The 3 Things the AI Listens For

### 1. No Long Pauses
The AI penalizes pauses longer than about 3 seconds within the reading. A short natural pause between sentences is fine. Stopping mid-sentence to figure out a word is not.

**Fix:** Use your preparation time to scan for difficult words and work out how to say them before you start speaking.

### 2. All Words Read (Content Score)
Every word you skip, replace, or add costs you content marks. The AI compares what you said to the original text word by word.

**Fix:** Read every word. If you lose your place, find it quickly and continue — don't stop and restart.

### 3. Clear Vowel and Consonant Sounds (Pronunciation)
PTE's AI does not penalize Filipino, Indian, or other non-native accents. It scores whether your **phoneme production** is intelligible — not whether you sound British or American.

Common pronunciation errors that the AI catches:
- Dropping final consonants ("test" → "tes")
- Merging vowels ("bit" and "beat" sounding the same)
- Stress on the wrong syllable ("phoTOgraphy" instead of "phoTOgraphy")

## How to Use Your 35-Second Preparation Time

Don't just read silently. Use your prep time like this:

1. **Scan the whole passage** (first 5 seconds) — understand the topic and tone
2. **Mark difficult words** mentally — any technical terms, names, or long words
3. **Practice the first sentence silently** — the first few words set your pace
4. **Decide your reading speed** — aim for natural conversational pace, not slow and robotic

When the beep sounds, start immediately. Don't hesitate.

## Reading Speed: Slower Is Not Safer

Many test-takers read too slowly thinking it will improve clarity. The opposite is often true. Reading too slowly:
- Creates unnatural pauses that reduce fluency scores
- Makes you sound robotic, which can hurt pronunciation scores
- Risks running out of recording time on longer passages

Aim to read at the pace you would use to read something aloud to a friend — clear but natural.

## The Filipino Accent Advantage

PTE's AI scoring system is designed to recognize a wide range of accents. Filipino English speakers typically:
- Have clear consonant sounds
- Naturally stress syllables in ways that are intelligible to the AI
- Are comfortable reading aloud (common in Philippine education)

This means Filipino test-takers often perform **above average** on Read Aloud compared to other non-native speaker groups. Play to this strength.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting before the beep** — the recording won't capture it
- **Restarting after a mistake** — just keep going; restarting confuses the AI
- **Reading too quietly** — speak at a normal volume, not a whisper
- **Rushing the last sentence** — maintain pace throughout, don't speed up at the end

## Practice Drill

Take any paragraph from a news article or academic text. Set a 35-second timer for preparation, then record yourself reading it aloud. Play it back and check:
- Did you pause at any point for more than 2 seconds?
- Did you skip or change any words?
- Were your final consonants clear?

Do this daily for 2 weeks and your Read Aloud score will improve measurably.
